Classify the main mechanism that makes this exact claim misleading. Identify what the evidence actually disproves: a factual interpretation, a link between real events, or the existence of the claimed event.

KE (knowledge error): quote the factual proposition and identify the supplied reliable fact that contradicts it. Match the date, scope and units where relevant. An unusual scene or an unsupported suspicion is insufficient.
ES (event splicing): identify the claimed link and the verified origins of the real material. Show which event, time, place or actor the material belongs to and why the claimed link fails. A generic illustration or a visual inconsistency alone cannot establish two events.
EF (event fabrication): identify the core event presented as having occurred. Require affirmative evidence of fabrication, such as a reliable fact-check of that event, directly conflicting records, or verified construction evidence available in this request. Missing coverage, an unfamiliar event, or an edited image alone cannot establish EF. A clearly disclosed fictional scene or demonstration does not by itself assert a real event.

Boundary example (guidance only): an archive caption dating a photo to an earlier flood can disprove its attribution to today's flood. It does not establish that today's flood never happened. Compare ES with EF using that distinction. A wrong factual explanation of a documented event can support KE if that explanation is the target.
If the evidence verifies the claim as genuine and error attribution is inapplicable, use not_applicable with empty labels. If applicable but no unique mechanism meets its threshold, use UNKNOWN alone and name the evidence needed to decide. Preserve the same target throughout.
