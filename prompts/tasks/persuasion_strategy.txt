Consider all nine strategies jointly. Select each strategy for which a particular phrase, image detail or combination performs the persuasive function. Explain each selected cue briefly. Descriptions below are decision aids; examples are never evidence for the sample.

SOCIAL_PROOF uses other people's endorsement, use or testimony as a reason to trust a claim. Test: whose agreement is offered as evidence? BANDWAGON uses popularity to press the audience to join, conform or avoid being left behind. Test: what is the reader pushed to do because others do it? "Thousands of customers vouch for it" supports credibility; "Everyone has signed up; don't be the last" pressures conformity. A crowd or a count by itself establishes neither function. Select both when both functions are evidenced.
STORYTELLING conveys a point through characters and a temporal or causal sequence. Identify the sequence and the point it serves. NARRATIVE_ENGAGEMENT deliberately holds attention through suspense, delayed revelation, perspective-taking or a similar device. Identify the device and what keeps the audience engaged. A straightforward account can tell a story without a hook; "What she found behind the door will be revealed tomorrow" can create suspense without telling a developed story. Include both when the structure and attention device are evident.

POLARIZATION reinforces opposition between political camps. AUTHORITY uses an expert, institution or credential to lend credibility. RHETORICAL_QUESTION uses a question with an implied answer or stance. LOGICAL_REASONING connects premises or comparisons to a conclusion, even if the argument is unsound. EMOTIONAL_APPEAL uses an identifiable emotional cue to shape judgment or action. For each, explain how the cue advances the message; a politician, credential, question mark, number or sad event by itself is insufficient.
Return UNKNOWN alone when no strategy is sufficiently supported. A truth judgment or a guess about the author's motive supplies no strategy evidence.
